# Anchor 1.5 In-Product Copy & Register Guidelines

## Register Boundary

Anchor uses two related but distinct voices.

### Acquisition Register
Used in App Store metadata, screenshots, landing pages, paid/social creative, and short-form hooks. It may be more declarative, compressed, and provocative.

*Example:*
> Symbols bypass the conscious mind.

This language earns attention. It does not define the rhythm of the product itself.

### In-Product Register
Used throughout onboarding, Sanctuary, creation, Practice, completion, Thread Strength, paywall, empty states, and system copy.

It is direct, instructional, and mechanism-aware. It tells the user what to do and, where necessary, why that action matters.

The eight reference sentences below govern **in-product copy only**.

---

## Eight Reference Sentences

1. **The thesis**:
   > Repetition programs the subconscious by returning your attention to the same intention until it becomes easier to recognize the moment to act.

2. **The mechanism**:
   > A symbol compresses an intention into a form you can return to without repeating the words.

3. **Plain instruction**:
   > Choose the anchor you will return to.

4. **Instruction with the why attached**:
   > Return to the symbol and hold your attention there. Repetition gives the subconscious one clear pattern to recognize.

5. **Session completion**:
   > That prime is complete. You reinforced the pattern one more time.

6. **Return after a lapse**:
   > The thread weakened while you were away. One session starts strengthening it again.

7. **The skeptic's objection**:
   > You do not have to believe in the symbol for it to work as a cue. You have to return to it consistently.

8. **The limit**:
   > Anchor does not make outcomes happen. It keeps your intention present so you can act on it.

---

## Register Rules

### Action → Mechanism, Not One Fixed Sentence Shape
The recurring conceptual pattern is **action → mechanism**.
It is **not** `instruction → semicolon → explanation`.

Vary sentence construction deliberately:
- Two short sentences
- One continuous sentence
- Direct instruction with no explanation when the action is self-evident
- Short explanatory clause when the mechanism is necessary

Do not allow the teaching register to create mechanically identical copy across the product.

### WITHHOLDING
**Definition**: If a meaningful action feels arbitrary without understanding its purpose, attach the mechanism in one short clause.

- Do **not** flag ordinary controls, settings toggles, navigation, dismiss actions, or self-explanatory interface behavior simply because they lack explanatory text.
- `WITHHOLDING` is reserved for moments where understanding the purpose materially improves the practice.

### Noun Discipline
Use nouns according to their established jobs:
- **intention** = what the user wants to direct themselves toward
- **anchor / symbol** = the visual form created from that intention
- **pattern** = what repetition reinforces
- **thread** = Thread Strength and its continuity over time
- **cue** = permitted when specifically describing the symbol's behavioral function

*Strictly avoid* introducing interchangeable metaphors such as `signal`, `frequency`, `message`, or `imprint` merely for variety.

### Formal Register (Uncontracted)
In-product copy is deliberately **uncontracted**:
- Use: `do not`, `does not`, `you will`, `it is`, `cannot`, `are not`
- Avoid: `don't`, `doesn't`, `you'll`, `it's`, `can't`, `aren't`

The formal construction supports the product's typography and instructional authority. Keep sentences plain and direct rather than ornate.

### Warmth Without Softness
Anchor acknowledges difficulty, lapses, uncertainty, or resistance without reassuring the user reflexively.
- Steady, not soothing.
- Avoid emotional cushioning, therapeutic reassurance, motivational cheerleading, guilt, or punishment language.

### Causality Boundary
- Anchor claims effects on: attention, repetition, recognition, recall, behavioral cues, and practice consistency.
- Anchor does **not** claim: thoughts attract external outcomes, symbols alter external reality, belief causes events, or visualization guarantees results.
- **The product primes action. It does not promise attraction.**
